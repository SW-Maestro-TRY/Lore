THE CHARACTER — a young adult man, a martial artist swordsman. Long black hair
tied high in a ponytail that streams behind him, sharp dark eyes, a slim angular
build with squared shoulders. He wears a wrap-front martial arts robe with a
contrasting inner collar, a swirling emblem on the left chest, a cloth waist belt
tied at the front, and dark trousers and cloth shoes. The attached image is his
official character sheet: use it ONLY for his face, hairstyle, build and costume
design. Do NOT copy its standing pose, its flat frontal posture, its layout, its
panels or its palette chips — this drawing is a completely different image.

A SINGLE DYNAMIC ACTION ILLUSTRATION. One instant of combat, drawn as one
continuous picture.

THE MOMENT: he is mid-attack, caught at the peak of the swing. He has just
launched off the ground and is cutting downward-diagonally with his straight
sword, the blade already past the point of impact and trailing its arc across the
frame. His torso twists hard, his body axis tilted steeply off vertical — nearly
diagonal across the picture. One leg drives back behind him, the other knee pulls
up. His leading arm and the sword come toward the viewer in strong foreshortening.
The ponytail and the sleeves and hem of his robe whip backward in the direction he
came from. Cloth and hair are stretched by the speed.

CAMERA: close and low, wide-angle. He DOMINATES the frame — his figure fills most
of the picture and parts of him are cropped by the edges. His face is turned
toward the viewer, large enough that the expression reads instantly: eyes
narrowed, brows driven down, jaw set, teeth showing with effort. This is not a
distant view. He is not small. He is not standing still. He is not centred in a
calm symmetrical composition.

OPPONENTS: at most two or three, and only as fragments at the edges of the frame —
a shoulder and arm being knocked backward out of the lower corner, a spear
spinning away, a half-cropped silhouette recoiling. The direction of the hit must
be obvious: something is being driven away from him along the line of his blade.
Never a crowd. Never ranks of soldiers. Never a formation standing and watching.

LINEWORK IS EVERYTHING: brush-pen strokes with strong pressure variation — each
stroke enters thick and tapers to a fine sharp point. Confident, clean, decisive
single strokes. Sharp and crisp, never uniform in width, never timid, never
scratchy or sketchy. The silhouette and the line direction carry the force.

WHITE SPACE: the paper is WHITE and largely EMPTY. Wide untouched white areas
around and behind him. That emptiness is the point — it is what makes the motion
read.

ALMOST NO TONE: no halftone screentone dots, no grey wash, no cross-hatched
shading fields, no large solid black areas, no black background. Black ink is used
only for tiny accents — pupils, the inside of the mouth, a few narrow slivers of
shadow inside a fold. When in doubt, leave it white.

MOTION MARKS ARE SUPPORT, NOT THE SUBJECT: a few long tapering speed lines
trailing the blade and the driving leg, one clean sweeping trail marking the path
the sword cut, a small scatter of debris or plum-blossom petals torn loose by the
movement. They must be sparser and quieter than the figure. The pose alone must
read as violent movement even if every effect line were erased.

BACKGROUND: essentially none. No building, no courtyard, no walls, no scenery, no
crowd, no ground plane. White void.

PROPORTIONS: a real adult body, about seven and a half heads tall, with correct
adult anatomy, long limbs and real weight. ABSOLUTELY NOT chibi, NOT
super-deformed, NOT SD, NOT a cute simplified figure, NOT short-limbed, NOT a big
head on a small body. This is a serious action drawing.

FORBIDDEN: panel borders, frames, gutters, multiple panels, a comic-book page
layout, a collage of several moments, speech bubbles, thought bubbles, caption
boxes, narration, sound-effect lettering, any letters or words in any language,
watermark, signature.

Not a manga page. Not a screentoned comic page. Not a coloured illustration. Not a
3D render. Not a rough sketch. ONE drawing of ONE action.

STYLE
  Dark noir webtoon panel — heavy chiaroscuro ink work, oppressive and
  threatening. One panel of an ongoing scrolling episode that continues above
  and below it, not a poster and not a standalone illustration. DARKNESS IS
  THE SUBJECT OF THIS PANEL, not its background. Most of the frame is solid
  black. The lit area is the minority and it is small, deliberate and shaped
  — a wedge of light across a face, a strip along a jaw, the top plane of a
  shoulder. Compose the panel as a design of black masses first and figures
  second: decide where the black shapes go, then let the drawing live in what
  is left. If the image reads as bright, or as a lit scene with some shadows
  in it, it is wrong. VALUE: crushed and limited to about four steps — solid
  black, a dark tone, a mid tone, and a small area of near-white. Nothing
  sits in between; the midtones are squeezed out. Shadows go completely black
  with no detail recovered inside them, and it is correct for a hand, a leg,
  half a torso or an entire secondary figure to be swallowed by the black and
  read only as a silhouette. Highlights blow to near-white with a hard edge.
  SHADOW SHAPE: the shadows are DRAWN, not simulated. Hard-edged, graphic,
  designed shapes with no soft falloff and no gradient at the boundary — cast
  slats from a blind striping a face and the wall behind it, the black cross
  of a window frame, the bars of a railing, the shadow of a doorway falling
  as one clean black wedge across the floor. Let the cast shadow cut a face
  in half so that only one eye and a strip of cheek stay lit. Where a soft
  edge is needed, it is an ink-wash bleed, not a lighting gradient.
  RENDERING: brush and ink. Solid black fills laid with a loaded brush, grey
  ink wash pooling and drying with visible hard edges, dry-brush drag where
  the brush runs out, spatter and flicked ink in the darkness, coarse
  hatching only where a form has to turn inside a shadow. The blacks are
  dense and flat, not airbrushed. Texture comes from ink and paper, never
  from a photographic grade. COLOUR: nearly desaturated. Cold near-black
  blue-greys and cool charcoals for almost everything, with warm tones
  drained out. Allow at most ONE restrained accent in the whole panel — a
  sodium-lamp amber, a sickly green, a dull blood red — used small and used
  once, on a sign, a cigarette, a wound, one eye. All other local colours
  collapse toward that near-monochrome scheme. Never a full colour panel,
  never bright saturated colour, never a warm pleasant palette. LINEWORK:
  heavy, angular and unequal — a thick brushed contour where a form meets the
  light, and no contour at all where it meets the black, because there the
  silhouette itself is the edge. Lines break, blot and taper. Never thin,
  never uniform, never clean vector inking. FACE: hard and angular, cut by
  shadow. Sharp cheekbones, a defined jaw, a strong brow ridge, deep hollows
  under the eyes. Most often the eyes sit inside a solid black band of shadow
  with only a hard specular catchlight showing — that glint is frequently the
  brightest thing in the panel. Half a face lit and half in absolute black is
  the default framing for a close-up here. Expressions are contained and
  cold: a flat stare, a small tightening of the mouth, a lowered brow. No
  large glossy eyes, no layered jewel iris, no blush, no sparkle, no cute
  expressions, no cartoon sweat drops. PROPORTION: grounded, heavy, adult —
  about seven to seven and a half heads tall, with real weight, thick
  shoulders, a low centre of gravity and believable balance. Bodies feel
  physically dangerous rather than elegant. No chibi outside an explicit SD
  panel, no long-legged glamour build. HANDS are drawn and lit deliberately:
  blocky and tendoned, gripping a weapon, a railing, a cigarette or someone's
  collar, usually with the fingers half lost in shadow and only the top
  planes catching light. HAIR: a single solid black mass with a sharp
  silhouette, cut by one or two hard-edged light strips where the key light
  rakes across it. No gloss ring, no soft gradient, no strand-by-strand
  rendering. ATMOSPHERE: wet, cold and enclosed. Rain, standing water
  throwing hard broken reflections, cigarette smoke and steam hanging in the
  light, dust in the air, fog rolling in the black. Environments are night
  streets, corridors, stairwells, basements, gate interiors, back alleys and
  rooms lit by one bad source. BACKGROUND: mostly consumed by black. Draw the
  little that has to be read — one lit doorway, one sign, a rail, the wet
  edge of a kerb, the geometry of a staircase — and let the rest go to solid
  ink. Use hard perspective and severe angles: low shots that make the figure
  loom, high shots that press them into the ground, tilted horizons. MOTION
  AND IMPACT: violent and graphic. Black smears and ink drag behind a moving
  limb, a hard white slash marking the path of a strike, a burst of spatter,
  harsh white impact cracks against black. Effects are made of ink, not of
  glow — no light trails, no particle sparkles, no bloom. Oppressive,
  threatening, heavy. NOT clean line art on white paper, NOT a bright panel,
  NOT cinematic photographic lighting — no depth of field, no bokeh, no lens
  flare, no soft light falloff, no film colour grading, no glow. No ornament,
  no roses, no gold, no sparkle. Not photorealistic, not a 3D render, not a
  grey painterly wash — brushed black ink and crushed value.

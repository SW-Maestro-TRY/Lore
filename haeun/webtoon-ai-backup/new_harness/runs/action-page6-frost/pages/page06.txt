  MONOCHROME INK FIRST:

  The image must be primarily white paper and black ink.

  Do NOT render the artwork as a grayscale painting.
  Do NOT fill the character with gray tones.

  Color/value is converted into ink through line density, hatching, shadow
  shapes, and solid black — NOT through continuous grayscale coloring.

  Use:
  - white paper for light areas
  - clean black linework for structure
  - solid black ink for deep shadows and dark original colors
  - sparse hatching for intermediate values
  - occasional very light gray only when absolutely necessary, and never as
    a flat fill

  The dominant visual impression must be BLACK INK ON WHITE PAPER. Large
  portions of the character should remain pure white.

  Do NOT evenly distribute gray across the character. Do NOT give the
  entire hair, cape, clothing, or skin a gray fill — that reads as a
  "colored painting" even in monochrome, which is exactly wrong.

  Dark original colors become stronger black ink and denser shadow/hatching
  areas. Light original colors stay mostly white, with black ink only at
  the shadow lines between strands/folds/forms. Mid-value original colors
  get selective hatching or small localized dark shapes, never a flat gray
  wash over the whole surface.

  IMPORTANT MONOCHROME INKING RULE (this is the part that fails most often):

  DO NOT use grayscale fill as a substitute for color. The artwork must NOT
  look like a grayscale colored illustration. For every character —
  regardless of whether their original hair, skin, or clothing color was
  light or dark — render the material primarily through BLACK INK AND WHITE
  PAPER.

  A medium or dark original color does NOT mean the entire surface should
  become a gray fill. Do not uniformly fill shirts, capes, hoods, hair,
  pants, or skin with gray, no matter how dark the original color was.

  Instead:
  - LIGHT AREAS: leave as clean white paper.
  - MID-VALUE AREAS: use linework, sparse hatching, and localized shadow
    shapes — not a wash.
  - DARK AREAS: use solid black ink selectively and structurally, following
    the actual form and folds of the object.
  - VERY DARK MATERIALS (black pants, dark hoods, dark hair): may contain
    larger solid-black areas, but must preserve enough white linework,
    highlights, fold lines, and silhouette separation to clearly show the
    material, its folds, and the anatomy underneath. A dark garment is
    built from black ink shapes with white gaps for form, not a single gray
    or black rectangle.

  GRAY MUST NEVER BECOME THE DOMINANT FILL COLOR. The viewer should perceive
  WHITE PAPER + BLACK INK first, with any gray only as a rare, secondary,
  localized technique — never a full-surface tone. A character with dark
  clothing should look INKED, not COLORED GRAY. Think like a traditional
  manga/webtoon ink artist working in pure line and black ink, not a
  grayscale painter softly shading a form.

  The uploaded character sheet is the ABSOLUTE REFERENCE for the character's
  identity and design (hairstyle, hair length, facial features, eye shape,
  body proportions, costume, accessories, weapons, distinctive elements) —
  but not for color, and not for how it is rendered. The sheet determines
  WHAT the character looks like; this style determines HOW it is drawn.

  Worked example — a character with light pink hair, a pink cape, a white
  dress, gold trim, black pants, pale skin, and pink eyes:
  - pink hair -> mostly white/paper, with black ink or fine hatching only in
    the shadows between hair strands and where locks overlap
  - pink cape -> mostly light/white, with black ink at the folds and the
    cape's inner-facing shadow
  - white dress -> stays almost entirely white, line only
  - gold trim -> the lit face of the metal stays white/paper, the metal's
    own shadow is black
  - black pants -> built mostly from solid black ink shapes since the
    original color is dark, but with white fold lines and highlight gaps
    left in so the legs, folds, and silhouette still read clearly — never
    one flat black rectangle
  - pale skin -> white, with only minimal black linework for shadow (under
    chin, hair shadow on face)
  - pink eyes -> this is the one part that stays actual color (see below)

  Worked example — a secondary character with a brown hood, a dark scarf, a
  brown/beige shirt, dark pants, and tan skin (no color sheet attached, but
  the same rule applies to every character in the panel, not only the one
  with a reference sheet):
  - brown hood -> mostly white/paper base, built from black contour lines
    and fold lines, with solid black only in the deepest folds/interior
    shadow — NOT a gray or brown-toned fill over the whole hood
  - dark scarf -> denser black ink than the hood (it's a darker original
    color), but still built as ink shapes with white gaps at folds and
    edges, not a single dark block
  - brown/beige shirt -> mostly white paper with black contour and fold
    lines, black ink concentrated only at armpit creases, overlaps, and
    where fabric bunches
  - dark pants -> same rule as the black pants above: solid black ink
    shapes with white fold lines kept visible
  - tan skin -> white/paper, with only minimal black linework for shadow
    (this does not change just because the character is secondary)

  A secondary or "less important" character gets simpler, sparser linework
  than the protagonist — fewer lines overall — but is never rendered with a
  gray fill as a shortcut. Less important still means white paper + black
  ink, just with less of it.

  INK AND LINEWORK:

  This is NOT a color illustration with a black-and-white filter, and it is
  NOT a flat grayscale-painted illustration either. Draw the scene as if a
  professional webtoon artist originally created it directly in monochrome
  ink on paper.

  Use: confident expressive ink lines, varied line weight, clean strong
  contours, delicate interior lines, detailed hair strands, detailed fabric
  folds, precise facial features, intricate costume details, carefully
  constructed hands, controlled solid black areas, selective hatching where
  it earns its place.

  Priority for this style, in order: character drawing quality (face, hair,
  hands, costume detail) far above everything else, then black linework and
  solid black shadow shapes, then sparse hatching for mid-tones, and last —
  minimal — background linework and any gray at all.

  The main character receives the most refined, detailed linework.
  Secondary characters and background receive lighter, sparser linework —
  fewer lines, not gray fill instead of lines.

  Do NOT make every surface equally detailed.
  Do NOT cover the entire image with hatching or gray.
  Do NOT make the background look like an architectural technical drawing —
  keep background linework light and sparse, secondary to the character.
  Leave large areas of pure white paper as intentional visual rest.

  The result should look like an artist carefully inked one finished
  webtoon page, not like an AI converted a colored image into a grayscale
  illustration.

  SELECTIVE COLOR:

  Color is an accent, NOT a rendering system. Only a tiny number of elements
  (commonly just the eyes; sometimes blood, a magical glow, a few petals, or
  a gemstone) retain actual, vivid color. Everything outside the selected
  accent(s) is strictly neutral: white paper, black ink, and — sparingly —
  hatching. No hue, no tint, no pastel version of the original color
  anywhere else.

  This is a technique for WHEN a point color is wanted, not a mandate that
  every panel must contain one. A fully monochrome panel with no color
  accent at all is also correct. If the scene does not specify an accent,
  the character's eyes are the safest default — but never force more than
  one or two accents, and never let color leak into hair, clothing, or
  weapons just because the sheet shows them in that color.

## 말풍선
- 지정된 한국어 대사를 말풍선 안에 정확히 그려 넣는다. 글자를 바꾸거나 빼지 않는다.
- 말풍선은 인물의 얼굴을 가리지 않는 위치에 둔다.
- 꼬리는 말하는 인물을 향한다. 지정이 있으면 그 방향을 따른다.
- 한 컷에 말풍선이 여러 개면 위에서 아래 순서로 읽히게 배치한다.
- 말풍선 모양:
  - 말: 둥근 타원
  - 생각: 구름 모양
  - 외침: 뾰족한 형태
  - 화면밖: 꼬리를 컷 바깥으로 향하게 하거나 꼬리를 없앤다
  - 전화·라디오: 각진 형태
  - 나레이션: 말풍선이 아니라 네모 상자. 컷 위쪽 모서리에 붙인다
  - 효과음: 말풍선 없이 글자만 그린다
- 글로 오가는 것(메일, 메시지, 문서)은 말풍선으로 그리지 않는다.
  화면이나 종이를 그리고 그 안에 글자를 넣는다.

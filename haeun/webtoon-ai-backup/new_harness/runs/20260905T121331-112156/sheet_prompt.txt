STYLE
  Korean slice-of-life webtoon (일상툰) drawn in a warm, hand-made, deliberately
  UNPOLISHED style — the look of a personal diary comic an author draws
  alone, not a studio production. It is one panel of an ongoing scrolling
  episode that continues above and below it. THE POINT OF THIS STYLE IS THAT
  IT LOOKS UNFINISHED. It must read as if the drawing were made quickly by
  one hand on real paper and left that way on purpose. Anything that makes it
  look expensive, corrected or mass-produced is wrong here. LINEWORK: rough,
  loose, visibly hand-drawn strokes with strong pressure variation — pencil,
  coloured pencil or a soft dry brush pen, never a crisp vector line. Lines
  wobble slightly, thicken and thin, and often overshoot at the corners.
  Shapes are frequently LEFT OPEN: a sleeve, a chair leg or a shoulder may
  simply stop, and the reader closes it. Some contours are drawn twice with
  the searching stroke still visible. The line is not pure black — it is a
  warm charcoal grey, a soft brown or a muted tone of the local colour. No
  inking-over, no perfectly closed outlines, no uniform digital weight.
  COLOUR: a limited muted palette of about five or six low-saturation tones —
  dusty pastels and warm greys, like faded coloured pencil, gouache or
  watercolour on tinted paper. Everything sits in a narrow value range with
  gentle contrast; nothing in the panel is fully saturated and nothing is
  pure white or pure black. The colour is laid in as SOFT UNEVEN PATCHES that
  do not line up with the drawing: it slips past the contour on one side,
  stops short of it on the other, and leaves small breathing gaps of bare
  paper inside the shape. Pigment pools slightly darker at the edge of a
  stroke. Do not fill areas flatly and do not use cel shading with a crisp
  shadow edge. TEXTURE: visible paper grain across the whole image, faint
  pencil tooth in the shaded areas, a slightly warm off-white paper tone
  underneath everything. The texture is part of the drawing, not a filter
  laid on top of a clean render. SHADOW: barely there. One soft muted tone
  dropped where it is genuinely needed — under the chin, under a table, on
  the side of a face away from the window — with a soft irregular edge, and
  nothing else. No rim light, no glow, no bloom, no shine, no specular
  highlights anywhere in the panel. FACE: small, simple and warm. The eyes
  are modest in size — a soft oval, a simply drawn iris, ONE small catchlight
  or none at all, a short lash line, no layered iris and no jewel rendering.
  The nose is a tiny stroke or dot, the mouth a short soft line. Cheeks get a
  small patch of dry blush that sits slightly off-register from the face.
  Expression is quiet and everyday: thinking, tired, mildly amused,
  distracted. Save big expressions for actual comedy beats, where they become
  a loose scribbled exaggeration rather than a rendered one. PROPORTION:
  adults about six and a half to seven heads tall — a little rounder, softer
  and shorter than the polished styles, with gentle sloping shoulders, a soft
  body line and no idealised V-taper or long-legged glamour. Never chibi
  outside an explicit SD panel, but never a fashion figure either. HANDS:
  drawn simply and honestly with four fingers and a thumb, a little chunky,
  correct in structure but not detailed — no tapering elegant fingers, no
  rendered knuckles. Hands doing small ordinary things (holding a mug, a
  phone, a pen, a bag strap) are welcome and should stay in frame. HAIR: a
  soft loose mass drawn with a few sweeping strokes and a scatter of stray
  hairs. The highlight, if any, is a single pale broken band or a patch of
  bare paper left uncoloured — never a glossy ring, never several bands,
  never strand-by-strand rendering. BACKGROUND: an ordinary lived-in place,
  drawn loosely but genuinely present — a classroom, a convenience store, a
  bus stop, a small kitchen, a stairwell at dusk. Perspective is slightly
  relaxed and hand-judged rather than ruled. Draw only a handful of specific
  everyday objects and let the rest fall away into bare paper: a half-drawn
  windowframe, three books, one mug ring on the desk. Small mundane detail is
  what carries the warmth here, so do not empty the panel completely — but
  never render the environment fully. COMPOSITION AND MOOD: quiet and
  observational. Plenty of empty paper around the figure, a lot of medium and
  wide shots where the character is small in a real room, ordinary eye-level
  framing. The feeling comes from the moment and the room, not from a
  dramatic angle. MOTION AND FEELING: carried by small hand-drawn marks that
  look scribbled in after the fact — a couple of short motion strokes, a
  little puff of dust, a sweat drop, three tiny lines over the head, a small
  hand-lettered scribble of surprise. Keep them sparse, wobbly and light. No
  dense radiating focus lines, no impact bursts filling the panel, no screen-
  toned effect fields. Warm, intimate, gentle, a little melancholy — the
  visual voice of a personal daily-life webtoon. NOT clean, NOT crisp, NOT
  cel-shaded, NOT glossy, NOT a cover illustration, NOT cinematic, no depth-
  of-field blur, no bokeh, no lens flare, no sparkle particles, no gold
  ornament, not photorealistic, not a 3D render, and not the polished weekly-
  serial webtoon look.

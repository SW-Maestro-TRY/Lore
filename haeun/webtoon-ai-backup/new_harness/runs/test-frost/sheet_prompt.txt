  Korean webtoon comic artwork, consistent serialized webtoon art style.

  CLEAN WEBTOON STYLE LOCK:

  The image must look like a finished panel from a professionally serialized
  Korean webtoon, not like an anime illustration, concept art, game artwork, or
  fantasy key visual.

  Use clean, controlled 2D digital lineart as the foundation of the artwork.
  Thin to medium-weight outlines with smooth, confident, deliberate strokes.
  Clear silhouettes and readable shapes.
  Lineart should remain visible and important throughout the image.

  Use clean flat colors with clearly separated color areas.
  Use simple cel shading with only a small number of shadow shapes.
  Keep shading shallow and economical.
  Use soft gradients only when absolutely necessary.
  Avoid complex multi-layer rendering.

  Characters should have attractive but natural Korean webtoon facial features.
  Expressive eyes and eyebrows.
  Delicate eyelashes.
  Simple, readable noses and lips.
  Facial expressions should communicate emotion clearly.
  Allow natural webtoon-style exaggeration of facial expressions when appropriate.

  Hair should be drawn as large coherent masses with a limited number of
  intentional strand lines.
  Do not render individual hairs.
  Hair should have simple color separation and restrained highlights.

  Clothing should use clear silhouettes and simplified folds.
  Show only the folds necessary to communicate the material, pose, and movement.
  Avoid excessive fabric texture or microscopic detail.

  Use simplified but convincing anatomy and natural proportions.
  Hands, faces, body poses, and gestures should remain readable and expressive.

  BACKGROUND STYLE:

  Backgrounds should support the scene rather than compete with the characters.
  Use simplified environmental shapes, clean perspective, and selective detail.
  Include enough environmental information to establish the location and
  atmosphere, but intentionally omit unnecessary details.

  Do not make every background object highly detailed.
  Distant people and objects may be simplified.
  Backgrounds should have lower visual complexity than the main characters.

  COLOR STYLE:

  Clean harmonious colors.
  Soft pastel-leaning colors when appropriate to the scene.
  Moderate saturation.
  Clear color separation.
  Limited color variation within each object.
  Avoid excessive gradients and color noise.

  LIGHTING:

  Simple, natural 2D webtoon lighting.
  One clear primary lighting direction.
  Simple shadow shapes.
  Subtle ambient light.
  No dramatic cinematic lighting.
  No excessive glow or volumetric effects.

  OVERALL VISUAL PRIORITY:

  1. Character acting and facial expression
  2. Clear lineart
  3. Character silhouette and pose
  4. Clean color blocks
  5. Simple cel shading
  6. Composition
  7. Background information

  The artwork should feel intentionally simplified and confident, not sloppy —
  but "confident" means economical drawing, not uniform polish. See "WEBTOON
  DRAWING ECONOMY" below.

  Beauty should come from character design, facial expression, line quality,
  composition, and color harmony, not from excessive rendering or visual
  effects.

  Maintain the same drawing language, line quality, shading method, color
  treatment, facial rendering, hair rendering, and background simplification
  throughout the entire image.

  The final result should feel like: "a real panel taken directly from a
  Korean webtoon episode." NOT: "an anime illustration made to look like a
  webtoon."

  WEBTOON DRAWING ECONOMY:

  The artwork must NOT look uniformly polished.

  Use intentional visual simplification throughout the panel.
  Not every object needs complete rendering.

  Prioritize the main character and the current action.
  Reduce detail progressively in secondary characters, background objects,
  architecture, decorations, and distant elements.

  Some areas should contain only simple shapes and a few lines.
  Do not fully render every visible object.

  Faces should use economical drawing.
  Do not perfect facial symmetry.
  Do not over-render the eyes.
  Do not add excessive eyelashes, iris details, highlights, or skin rendering.

  Hair should be drawn in broad masses with a few structural lines.
  Avoid individually describing every strand.

  Clothing should be communicated through silhouette and a small number of
  meaningful folds rather than complete fabric rendering.

  Backgrounds should feel like backgrounds in an actual comic panel:
  recognizable and atmospheric, but selectively unfinished and simplified.

  Allow subtle variation in line weight.
  Some contour lines may be lighter or disappear where appropriate.
  Do not make every contour equally crisp.

  The goal is not maximum visual polish.
  The goal is efficient visual storytelling.

  IMPORTANT: Do not make every part of the image equally beautiful or equally
  detailed.

  The viewer's eye should naturally move: main character → expression/action
  → supporting elements → background.

  The panel should feel drawn for sequential storytelling, not designed as a
  standalone illustration.

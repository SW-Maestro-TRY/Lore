STYLE
  Korean webtoon panel drawn in the shoujo / BL manga tradition (순정만화 · BL풍)
  — graphic, elegant and emotionally charged, built from line, screentone and
  empty space rather than from painted rendering. It is one panel of an
  ongoing scrolling episode that continues above and below it. WHAT THIS
  PANEL IS ABOUT IS THE FACE, AND THE CHARGE BETWEEN TWO PEOPLE. Frame
  accordingly: close and very close shots dominate — a face at three
  quarters, a pair of eyes across the full width, a mouth, a throat, a wrist
  caught by a hand. Wide establishing shots are rare and brief. FACE: sharp
  and angular, not round and not soft. A narrow pointed chin, a long clean
  jawline meeting the ear at a distinct angle, high flat cheekbones, a
  slender neck, a long straight nose reduced to one fine tapering stroke with
  a small shadow beside it, and a small mouth with a defined upper lip. EYES
  — long, narrow and elegant, NOT large round jewels. The eye is a
  horizontally elongated almond with a sharply upswept outer corner and a
  strong dark upper lash line that thickens toward that corner into a
  decisive wedge. The iris is tall and partly hidden under the upper lid.
  Inside it: one dark rim, a simple vertical value gradient rendered in dot
  tone, a solid dark pupil, ONE crisp white catchlight and at most one small
  secondary one. Long individually drawn upper lashes flick outward at the
  corner; the lower lash line is a few separate strokes. Never a wide
  circular eye, never a layered faceted gemstone iris, never three or four
  scattered sparkles inside it. EYEBROWS are thin, long and precisely shaped,
  and they do most of the acting. PROPORTION: elongated and elegant — about
  nine heads tall, with a small head, an extremely long neck, narrow sloping
  shoulders, a long waist, very long legs and slender limbs. Figures read as
  tall, weightless and stylised rather than anatomically grounded. Never
  squat, never chibi outside an explicit SD panel. HANDS are long, bony and
  expressive, with visible tendons and knuckles, elongated fingers and
  clearly drawn nails. Hands are a major subject in this style — a hand
  gripping a wrist, cupping a jaw, gathering a collar, hesitating just short
  of contact. Give them as much line work as the face and never crop them
  away. TWO FIGURES: the composition is about the distance between them and
  about who is above whom. Overlap the silhouettes, push one figure's
  shoulder or hair across the front of frame, tilt the framing so one looks
  down and the other up, and let a strand of hair, a breath of space or one
  hovering hand carry the tension. Keep the gap between them explicit and
  readable. SHADING — SCREENTONE, not painting. Model form with flat
  mechanical dot tone: an area of even halftone dots for a mid value, a
  graded dot ramp where a plane turns, a denser tone for a deep shadow, and
  hard white where the light lands. Tone areas have crisp cut edges, exactly
  as if a sheet of tone had been cut with a blade, and may be scraped white
  with fine parallel scratch lines at the highlight. Use tone gradients
  behind and around the figures as pure mood. No airbrushed skin, no soft
  painted gradients, no volumetric light. COLOUR: restrained and desaturated.
  A narrow palette of pale neutrals, dusty roses, cool greys, ivory and ink,
  held at low saturation across the whole panel, with tone standing in for
  most of the shading. Exactly ONE saturated accent is allowed per panel —
  the eyes, a ribbon, a single flower, blood on a lip — and it must be the
  only place the eye goes. Large areas of flat white and flat black are
  correct and desirable. Never full-colour ornate rendering, never a glossy
  pastel gradient wash. LINEWORK: fine, sharp and strongly modulated — a
  hairline stroke swelling and then tapering to a needle point, drawn with a
  G-pen. Long unbroken confident strokes for hair and cloth, delicate strokes
  for the face. Line weight varies hard between the figure's silhouette and
  its interior detail. Pure black line, never coloured line. HAIR: the most
  decorative element. Drawn as long flowing separated ribbons of strands,
  streaming outward as if lifted by a wind that exists only for emotional
  effect, with fine loose hairs breaking away at the edges. Inside the mass,
  individual strands are picked out with sharp tapering lines. Fill it with
  flat black or flat tone, and cut the highlight in as a sharp-edged white
  shape or a scratched band, never as a soft glossy gradient ring. DECORATIVE
  MOTIFS IN THE NEGATIVE SPACE — this is a defining feature. Where the
  background is dropped, fill the empty space with drawn graphic motifs
  floating around the figures: line-drawn roses, lilies and camellias with
  falling petals, four-pointed sparkle glints, small bubbles and circles,
  feathers, ribbons, and a wash of dot tone behind them. They are FLAT
  GRAPHIC SYMBOLS drawn in ink and tone on the surface of the panel — not
  lens bokeh, not glowing 3D particles, not gold foil ornament, not lace or
  embroidery. BACKGROUND: frequently dissolved entirely and replaced by tone,
  motifs and white space, especially in emotional close-ups. When a real
  place is shown, it is drawn cleanly with fine architectural line and a
  little tone, then stopped early — the setting is stated, never rendered in
  full. Vertical composition, empty space above the head, and severe diagonal
  framing are all encouraged. EXPRESSION AND MOOD: intense but held back. A
  downward gaze, a half-lidded look, a trembling lash line, one line of
  shadow across the eyes, a bitten lip, a single tear cutting the cheek.
  Feeling is enormous but the face barely moves. Avoid loud comic expressions
  and cartoon sweat drops in a normal panel. Graphic, elegant, tense,
  romantic. NOT a romance-fantasy cover illustration — no faceted jewel eyes,
  no gold filigree, no lace or beading rendered individually, no rose-and-
  bokeh painted background, no full-colour ornament. Not photorealistic, not
  a 3D render, not a soft painted illustration, not a plain flat weekly
  webtoon panel.

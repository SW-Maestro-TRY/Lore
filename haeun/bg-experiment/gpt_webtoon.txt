THIS IS A WEBTOON PANEL, NOT AN ILLUSTRATION.

This overrides every other rendering instruction above, including anything the
shot description implies. It describes HOW the panel is drawn, not what is in it.

The single most common failure here is drawing a beautiful cover illustration
instead of a comic panel. This is one ordinary panel of a weekly serialised
Korean webtoon, drawn on a schedule, read while scrolling on a phone. It is not
a poster, not key art, not concept art, not a book cover, not a wallpaper.

LINEWORK — THE PANEL MUST HAVE VISIBLE LINES. Every object, every figure, every
piece of background architecture is enclosed by a drawn outline of nearly uniform
weight, with a slightly heavier contour around the main character. Shapes are
fully closed. NEVER define a form by colour alone, by a soft edge, or by a value
change with no line — that is painting, and it is the single thing that makes
these panels read as illustration instead of comic. If you can remove the colour
and still read the drawing, the linework is right.

COLOUR — CEL SHADING ONLY. Each area gets ONE flat base tone, plus ONE shadow
tone, plus at most ONE light tone. The tones meet at a HARD boundary — a crisp
edge, not a blend. Forbidden everywhere in the panel: gradient fills, airbrushed
transitions, soft blended shading, glow layers, texture overlays, painterly
brushwork, colour that fades out toward an edge. A large area of one flat colour
is correct and expected.

NO PHOTOGRAPHIC OR CINEMATIC LIGHT. No sparkle particles, no glitter, no bokeh,
no light bloom, no lens flare, no god rays, no rim light, no specular glints on
water or metal, no depth-of-field blur, no atmospheric haze or mist, no soft
vignette. Light is drawn as one flat lighter shape with a hard edge, or it is
not drawn at all.

BACKGROUND. Simple and flat: a plain colour field, a flat gradient-free tone, a
screen-tone dot or line pattern, or a sparsely drawn setting containing only the
objects the scene actually needs — each of those objects outlined like everything
else. Water is flat shapes with a few drawn wave lines, never a rendered
reflective surface. Sky is a flat tone, never a photographic gradient. The
background is always quieter than the character and never competes with them.

WEBTOON GRAMMAR — DRAW THE COMIC FURNITURE. This is a comic, so it has the parts
a comic has, and they are DRAWN OBJECTS with outlines, not text floating on art:
  · Speech is inside a speech bubble: a closed drawn shape with a visible outline
    and a tail pointing at the speaker. A shout gets a spiky burst bubble; an
    inner thought gets a soft cloud bubble with small trailing circles.
  · A narration caption sits in its own rectangular box with a visible border,
    or on a clean flat band — not set loose on top of the artwork.
  · An inset or close-up detail sits inside its own panel frame with a drawn
    border, overlapping the main image.
  · Korean sound effects are hand-drawn display lettering integrated into the
    scene, with an outline or a contrasting edge so they stay readable.
  · Motion and emotion are drawn marks: speed lines behind a moving limb,
    radiating focus lines, small impact bursts, sweat drops, vertical shadow
    lines down a face. Use them — that is how this medium shows feeling. They sit
    on top of the drawing and never cover the figure or fill the panel.

TREAT THE SHOT DESCRIPTION AS MOOD, NOT AS A RENDER ORDER. The description above
is written in film language and will say things like "dawn light on high waves",
"shimmering", "atmospheric", "the water glitters". Those words say how the moment
should FEEL. Do not render them literally: no actual shimmer, no actual glitter,
no cinematic haze. Translate the feeling into flat shapes, hard-edged tones and
drawn effect marks.

Everyday commercial webtoon quality — clean, flat, instantly readable at a glance
on a phone. Not a painted illustration, not photorealistic, not a 3D render, not
ornately detailed.

BACKGROUND — DRAW A PICTURE BOOK, NOT A PLACE.

This overrides every other background instruction above, including anything in
the shot description. The character is already right; only the world behind and
around it changes.

FLAT DEPTH. There is no atmospheric perspective in this drawing. No haze, no
mist, no fog, no smog, no dust in the air, no depth-of-field, no distance blur,
no fading toward the horizon. Something far away is drawn with exactly the same
line and the same clarity as something near — it is only smaller, and carries
fewer details. Never soften a far object to push it back.

SHAPE, NOT TEXTURE. Every element behind the character is reduced to a handful
of simple drawn shapes with a visible outline. Concretely:
  · A beach is one or two flat bands of colour. It is NOT grains of sand, not
    scattered pebbles, not a speckled surface.
  · Water is a few flat shapes with two or three drawn wave lines on top. It is
    NOT a reflective rippling surface, not glittering, not mirroring the sky.
  · Wood is a shape with at most two or three drawn plank lines. It is NOT
    weathered, not grainy, not splintered, not stained.
  · Rock, stone, brick, foliage, fabric: three or four drawn shapes standing in
    for the whole mass. Never a field of repeated texture.
  · Sky is one flat wash, or two flat bands meeting at a soft edge. Never a
    photographic gradient, never rendered clouds.
No paper grain, no canvas tooth, no noise, no scanned-texture overlay anywhere
in the background.

COUNT LIMIT. At most four or five named things exist behind the character. If
the shot description lists more than that, keep the ones the story actually
needs and silently drop the rest. An emptier background is always the safer
reading of this instruction.

LEAVE IT UNFINISHED. A real part of the background — often the top, the edges,
or the ground far from the character — is left as plain flat colour with nothing
drawn on it at all. Empty space is the point of this look, not a mistake to fill.

NO PHOTOGRAPHIC LIGHT. No sun glare, no god rays, no lens flare, no bloom, no
specular sparkle on water or metal, no rim light, no bokeh, no vignette, no
golden-hour gradient doing the work of the drawing. Light is shown, if at all,
as one flat lighter shape and one flat darker shape.

SAME HAND. Background objects are outlined with the same visible hand-drawn line
as the character, in the same weight and the same colour family. The background
must never look airbrushed, painted or rendered while the character is drawn —
that split is exactly what makes an image read as realistic.

QUIETER THAN THE CHARACTER. The character is always the most saturated and most
detailed thing in the panel. The background sits lower in both, so the eye lands
on the character first, every time.

TREAT THE SHOT DESCRIPTION AS MOOD, NOT AS A RENDER ORDER. The description above
is written in film language and will say things like "extreme wide shot",
"shrouded in mist", "weathered pier", "soft morning light catching the water",
"atmospheric". Those words describe how the moment should FEEL. Do not render
them literally: no actual mist, no actual weathering, no actual glinting water,
no cinematic haze. Translate the feeling into flat shapes and quiet colour.

The finished panel should look like a page from a children's picture book or a
gentle Korean webtoon — drawn, flat, calm, with generous empty space — and never
like a photograph, a matte painting, a concept-art landscape or a 3D render.
